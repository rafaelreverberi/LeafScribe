A Field Guide
to Close Reading

A small book about paying attention.

01 / Begin with a question

Reading becomes more useful when you know what you are looking for. Before the first page, write down one question. It can be precise or open-ended: What changed? Why does this argument matter? What am I missing?

Your question is a thread, not a rule. Follow it until the book offers a better one.

02 / Leave a trace

A highlight marks a moment of attention. A note tells your future self why that moment mattered. Try pairing each important highlight with a short sentence in your own words.

Do not collect sentences simply because they sound good. Keep the ones that help you think.
When you noticeTry writing
An unfamiliar idea“This means…”
A surprising claim“I expected…, but…”
A connection“This reminds me of…”

03 / Make room to return

After a chapter, close the book. Write three things you remember and one thing you would like to understand better. The pause makes space for your own thinking.

Keep your notes close to the source. Export a PDF when page layout matters, or an EPUB when you want the text to adapt to your screen. A useful library is one you can return to.
